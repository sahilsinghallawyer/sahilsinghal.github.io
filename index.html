Fraulex — decoding fraud patterns

Fraulex is an independent research project. It studies how corporate fraud is constructed and how enforcement cases are built by regulators, then shares that analysis as self-study material sector guides, decoded case studies, and reference material a reader can work through on their own.

Research focus

Understanding how fraud is built, hidden, and prosecuted.

My research looks at the architecture of corporate fraud not just what happened, but how misconduct develops inside institutional systems, why controls fail under pressure, and how the same patterns recur across sectors and jurisdictions. It draws only on public enforcement records.

Corporate fraud architecture

How financial misconduct is structured: the layering, concealment, and gradual normalisation of irregular behaviour.

Enforcement case analysis

Major regulatory cases studied for what was found and what evidence proved decisive.

Control failure patterns

Why internal controls fail under real operational pressure, and how fraud exploits approval chains under urgency.

Genuine vs. paper compliance

How courts and regulators distinguish real fraud prevention from documentation built only to look adequate.

Cross-jurisdiction trends

How enforcement behaviour is evolving across India, the UK, and the US, and what that signals for compliance globally.
